Red Apple Care.

Care that feels personal, presented properly.

Engagement

Home care website & digital direction

Disciplines

Positioning, UX/UI, responsive design, service discovery

The intention

Give a UK home care provider a website that reassures families and honours the people it supports

View live site

Red Apple Care

01 / The challenge

Real care.
A website that didn't reassure.

Red Apple Care provides home care and support services across the UK — personal care, companionship, dementia support, respite care, and live-in care. The team is genuinely person-centred: they take time to understand the people they support, they build long-term relationships, and they treat families with the same respect they extend to service users. In a sector that's often criticized for coldness and turnover, Red Apple Care had built something rare: a care provider whose staff actually stay, and whose clients actually recommend them. The website did not reflect any of that. It was a stock-template home care site with generic smiling stock photography, service descriptions that could have been written by any competitor, and a "get in touch" form with no clear explanation of what would happen next. For families making one of the most emotionally difficult decisions of their lives — choosing who will care for a parent or partner — the site offered nothing reassuring. It read like a corporate brochure, when the actual business behaves like a trusted neighbour. The brief was to build a website that reflected the warmth and competence of the actual team.

02 / The approach

Warm, human,
dignified by default.

We built the direction around three decisions. First, a warm, human aesthetic — deep red, soft cream, and muted teal accents with a warm serif for headings and a clean sans for the interface. It reads like a trusted local care provider, not like a corporate chain and not like a hospital brochure. Second, the structure follows how families actually choose care. The homepage leads with a clear promise, an honest explanation of what Red Apple Care does and who it supports, and a "how to get started" section that walks through the first steps in plain English. There's no jargon, no referral funnel, and no pressure to make contact before you're ready. Third, the content centres the people, not the brand. Team members appear throughout the site with real names, roles, and short reflections on why they do the work. Service user stories are handled with dignity — no exaggerated before-and-after framing, no emotional manipulation. The site treats the reader with the same respect the team treats clients. The copy is plain, warm, and direct. "Care that feels personal" — that's the promise, and every page delivers on it. No "empowering independence" or "person-centred pathways." Just honest language about what home care actually looks like.

Warm, human design system with red/cream/teal palette

Dedicated service pages with plain-English "how to get started" flows

Team and care stories told with dignity, not exaggeration

03 / The build

Built for
real decisions.

The build is a static site with dedicated service pages (personal care, companionship, dementia support, respite care, live-in care), a proper "how to get started" section on each page, an about page with team members and their reflections, a "choosing care" guide that helps families understand what to look for and what questions to ask, a CQC registration and standards block for transparency, and a contact page with a warm enquiry form. Every page reinforces the same promise — that Red Apple Care sees the person, not the care package. Motion is warm but restrained. Hover states on service cards and CTAs are subtle. The header solidifies on scroll. Team photos fade in as they load. Accordions open smoothly. Every animation respects prefers-reduced-motion, and nothing in the motion layer gets in the way of a family finding the information they need. The result is a website that behaves the way Red Apple Care behaves: warm, respectful, and quietly excellent.

In care, dignity is the whole design brief.
