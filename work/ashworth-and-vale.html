Ashworth & Vale

01 / The challenge

A serious firm.
A digital first impression to match.

Ashworth & Vale is a boutique commercial law firm founded in 2009, based in Holborn. It handles corporate work, commercial litigation, employment, real estate, and private client matters — the kind of practice where the partner you meet on the first call is the partner who actually runs your case. Senior-led, no junior handoffs, fixed fees, plain English. It sits deliberately between the white-shoe City firms (too expensive, too impersonal) and the cheap online legal services (too thin, too risky). That positioning is genuinely sharp. The website didn't reflect it. It had been assembled piece by piece, with inconsistent typography, a legacy practice-areas menu that hadn't been revisited in years, and partner bios that read like LinkedIn summaries. Nothing about it said "the firm you actually want on your side." For a firm whose entire pitch is judgement and clarity, the site was doing neither — and that gap was costing them enquiries from the exact client profile they were best positioned to serve.

02 / The approach

Paper and ink.
Authority without stuffiness.

We rebuilt the direction around the look and feel of a well-printed legal brief — warm paper backgrounds, deep ink typography, brass accents used sparingly, Libre Caslon Display for headlines paired with Public Sans for the interface. The site feels like it could be printed on good stock. That matters for a firm competing on judgement, because visual restraint is read as competence in this category, whether consciously or not. The structure follows the same discipline. The homepage hero is type-led, not photo-led — the firm's headline carries the page rather than a stock photograph of a gavel. The practices section uses editorial case-note blocks instead of feature grids, each framed as a real matter type rather than a marketing category. The team page treats partners as first-class content — portraits, credentials, specialisms, direct email — because in boutique law, people really are the product. And the fixed-fee language runs through every practice page, because it's the sharpest differentiator the firm has and most competitors bury it.

03 / The build

Refined motion.
Editorial pace.

The build is a static site with a custom motion layer written in vanilla JavaScript — no libraries, no frameworks. Headlines reveal line by line as they scroll into view. Practice-area ornament lines draw themselves in. Stat counters count up when they enter the viewport. The page header solidifies on scroll. Everything is deliberate, everything respects prefers-reduced-motion, and nothing distracts from the words — which, for a law firm, is the right priority. The full site includes four detailed practice pages, a full team index with filter tabs (Partners / Senior Associates / Associates), an insights editorial index with search and category filters, a contact page with a clear enquiry form, and a small regulatory note block that signals the firm takes its obligations seriously. The result reads the way the firm wants to be read: senior, considered, human, and quietly confident.

In law, restraint is read as competence.
