Ironwell

01 / The challenge

Serious training.
Soft website.

Ironwell Athletic is a strength and conditioning studio in Clerkenwell, founded in 2019. Small-group training capped at eight, coach-led programming, a community of ex-athletes and serious amateurs who actually train — not a boutique fitness chain, not a CrossFit box, not a Globo-gym. The gym floor has chalk on it. The coaching is real. Members stay for years because the training works and the culture is worth showing up for. The website, by contrast, looked like every other gym in London. Soft pastel gradients, stock photos of smiling people on treadmills, and copy that used the word "journey" four times on the homepage. For a studio whose entire pitch is earned strength, the site was actively undermining the brand. It attracted the wrong enquiries — people looking for a boutique yoga studio rather than a training space — and it failed to convert the serious lifters who were the actual target audience. The brief was direct: build a website that sounds like the gym floor.

02 / The approach

Industrial. Type-led.
No wellness language.

We built the direction around three decisions. First, the aesthetic is industrial and type-led — near-black backgrounds, bone text, signal orange accents, and Anton (an ultra-condensed display sans) used at huge sizes for headlines. It reads like a gym poster or a strength training manual, not a wellness brand. Second, the site is dark-only by design. No light-mode toggle. That's a brand decision: the gym is dark, the chalk is white, the plates are black, and the site should look like the space it's describing. Third, the copy is direct, gritty, and free of every wellness cliché. The homepage carries a manifesto strip in huge Anton type — "We coach. / You work. / You get strong." — that has nothing to do with "self-care" or "your fitness journey." It sounds like a coach, because the brand is a coach. The structure follows the same logic. The training pages present programs (small-group, 1:1, hybrid) with real sample weeks, real session breakdowns, and real "who it's for" descriptions. The coaches page treats the team as the product — credentials, philosophy, portraits — because for a serious studio, the coaches are the entire reason someone joins. Membership pricing is upfront, comparison tables are honest, and the contact page asks for a trial session rather than a "discovery call."

03 / The build

Built for
serious lifting.

The build is a static site with three detailed training program pages, a coaches page with a head-coach feature and coach grid, a membership page with three tiers and an honest comparison table, and a contact page with a trial session form that asks for experience level and preferred training times. The marquee strip runs at the top of every page, reinforcing the brand voice on every scroll. The site is dark-only by design, and every page carries the same signal-orange accent. Motion is confident but restrained. The manifesto strip on the homepage staggers line-by-line as it scrolls into view. The marquee runs continuously at the top of every page (pausable, respects reduced motion). Stat counters count up. The primary CTA buttons have a subtle magnetic hover effect on desktop. Every animation respects prefers-reduced-motion, and nothing in the motion layer softens the gym's actual character. The result is a website that behaves the way the studio behaves: direct, uncompromising, and quietly excellent.

If the training is serious, the website should be too.
