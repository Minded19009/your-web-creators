Meridian

01 / The challenge

Serious work.
A website that looked like everyone else's.

The Meridian Trust has been working on clean water, sanitation, and women's economic empowerment in Sub-Saharan Africa and South Asia since 2011. It is a serious organization: long-term partnerships with local NGOs, rigorous impact measurement, low overheads, and a public transparency rating that most charities would envy. The work is real. The results are measurable. The communities it works with are treated as partners, not beneficiaries. The website, unfortunately, didn't reflect any of that. It read like a legacy charity site — a wall of stock imagery designed to shock, scattered impact statistics with no source or date, and a "donate" button that felt like an emotional ambush rather than an invitation. For an organization whose entire positioning is dignity and evidence, the site was doing exactly what the sector is criticized for. It was also failing the people it served: the communities Meridian works with were rendered as anonymous and pitiable, when in reality they are the partners running the projects.

02 / The approach

Warmth. Evidence.
Dignity by default.

We rebuilt the direction around three decisions. First, the photography and language were chosen to convey dignity, not pity. Community portraits, water infrastructure shots, women in workshops — warm, natural, respectful. Every image carries a specific caption naming the community and the project. No "crying child" imagery, no "saviorism" language, no stock photos of anonymous hands holding water. The tone is what a serious development organization actually sounds like. Second, the impact data is treated as a first-class design element. The impact page includes an animated stacked-bar chart showing where the money goes (programs 84%, operations 9%, fundraising 7%) — built in CSS and SVG, no chart libraries. There's a full breakdown of programs (water 52%, sanitation 28%, empowerment 20%), 4 large stat counters, downloadable annual reports, and an independent evaluation quote. Third, the donation experience is honest. The homepage carries a live donation calculator that shows exactly what £10, £25, £35, £75, or £150 funds. The donate page itself uses a one-off/monthly toggle, an amount selector with real-time impact updates, and a gift-aid checkbox. Nothing hidden, nothing manipulative — because for an evidence-led charity, transparency is the fundraising strategy.

03 / The build

Transparency
as a design language.

The build is a static site with a working donation calculator on the homepage, an interactive donation page with a one-off/monthly toggle and real-time impact updates, three detailed program pages (clean water, sanitation, women's empowerment) with their own inline-SVG maps of the regions served, an impact page with CSS/SVG data visualizations, an about page with the board of trustees, and a contact page with a clear enquiry form. The newsletter signup, donation flow, and contact form all use client-side validation and honest success messages — no simulated submission, no dark patterns. Motion is warm and restrained. The homepage hero uses a Ken Burns zoom on the primary photograph. Stat counters count up as they enter the viewport. The stacked-bar data visualizations animate their widths in sequence. The sticky donation nudge appears quietly after the user has scrolled 60% of the page. The pull-quote band rotates slowly. Every animation respects prefers-reduced-motion, and nothing in the motion layer gets between a visitor and the information that would earn their trust. The result is a charity website that behaves the way the charity itself does: warm, evidence-led, and genuinely transparent.
