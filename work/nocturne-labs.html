Nocturne

01 / The challenge

A serious studio.
A crowded category.

Nocturne Labs operates in the most crowded positioning space of the moment: AI. Every week, another consultancy launches a website claiming to "transform" businesses with "AI-powered solutions." The category has become almost comically uniform — the same blue gradients, the same overpromised copy, the same abstract renders of glowing neural networks that communicate nothing specific. Nocturne Labs is not that. It's a small, senior, seriously technical practice building AI agents, workflow automation, and process intelligence systems for operations-heavy companies. They ship production systems, not slides. The brief was straightforward in concept but difficult in execution: build a site that reads as *different* — not by shouting louder, but by saying less and saying it better. The site had to feel like the studio: quiet, confident, technically literate, and clearly expensive in taste.

02 / The approach

Dark. Editorial.
Deliberately different.

We made three deliberate choices that diverge from the rest of the category. First, the visual direction is dark-first, editorial, and heavily typographic — near-black backgrounds, a warm bone palette, an electric chartreuse accent used sparingly, and Instrument Serif as the hero typeface. It reads like a design studio site or a serious publication, not like a SaaS landing page. Second, the copy avoids every AI cliché. No "revolutionary," no "cutting-edge," no "unlock the power of AI." Instead: "automation for the work that matters," "we ship systems, not slides," "built for the last mile of the workflow." Third, motion was treated as a design language, not decoration — restrained, expensive-feeling, and always in service of the page. The result is a site that doesn't just describe the studio. It demonstrates the studio. If the pitch is craft and rigour, the site has to embody it.

03 / The build

Every detail
earned its place.

The build is a static site — HTML, CSS, vanilla JavaScript only — with a custom motion layer built entirely by hand. No animation libraries, no frameworks. The page-load intro, the full-screen mobile menu with staggered link reveals, the custom cursor that scales on interactive elements, the magnetic button effect on primary CTAs, the SVG path-drawing animations on flow diagrams, and the scroll progress indicator are all written in a single, carefully organized `script.js` file. Everything respects `prefers-reduced-motion` gracefully. Nothing moves without a reason. The site is fast, accessible, and fully responsive. It's the kind of website that rarely gets described as "impressive" by people who don't build websites, and gets described as "very good" by people who do. That was the goal.

Restraint is the most expensive material in the room.
