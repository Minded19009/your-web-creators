Ledgerline

01 / The challenge

Real product.
Website that didn't sell it.

Ledgerline is a revenue operations platform — pipeline analytics, forecasting, and billing intelligence for B2B companies between $5M and $100M ARR. Founded in 2022 in San Francisco, backed by a real Series A, and quietly winning against much larger incumbents in a category that's still being defined. The product works. The team ships. The customers renew. All the fundamentals of a serious SaaS company were in place. The website was the weak link. It had been built by the founders in a weekend, then patched together by three different agencies over eighteen months, and the result was a Frankenstein of inconsistent typography, contradictory positioning, and product screenshots that had gone stale. For a company whose entire pitch was "one source of truth for revenue," the website had become the opposite. It was actively losing deals — prospects were arriving on calls confused about what Ledgerline actually did, which meant the sales team was spending the first ten minutes of every demo re-explaining the product rather than selling it.

02 / The approach

Product-led.
Not marketing-led.

We rebuilt the site around three decisions. First, product-led — every page leads with the actual interface. Custom SVG dashboards show the pipeline view, the forecast view, and the billing view with real data and real workflows. No stock photography, no abstract renders, no "digital transformation" language. The product is the argument, and the site never lets you forget it. Second, positioning discipline. Ledgerline isn't for everyone — it's for RevOps teams, sales leaders, and CFOs at B2B companies in a specific ARR band. Every page speaks to that audience, and nothing on the site tries to appeal to the wrong buyer. Third, a confident voice. The copy is sharp and occasionally funny in a dry, B2B way. It says "the anti-bloated RevOps platform" on the homepage, which most SaaS vendors would never dare to write — and that's exactly why it works. The design language is deep indigo with emerald and amber accents, Sora for headings and Inter for the interface. It reads like a modern product company (Linear, Vercel, Stripe) rather than like an enterprise software brochure. That positioning alone does enormous work in a category where every competitor looks the same.

03 / The build

Every dashboard
built by hand.

The build is a static site with an interactive tabbed feature explorer on the homepage — clicking a tab (Pipeline, Forecasting, Billing, Reporting) swaps in a different SVG dashboard mockup and a short description — plus a features page with a category sidebar, a customer stories index, a pricing page with a sticky comparison table, an integrations page with 24 filterable integration cards, and a blog with search and category filters. All the mockups are hand-built inline SVGs, which keeps the site fast and makes every visual element consistent with the brand. Motion is used sparingly but intelligently: animated stat counters, smooth accordion transitions, a dismissible announcement bar at the top of the page, subtle hover lifts on cards and CTAs, and a header that solidifies on scroll. Every animation respects prefers-reduced-motion. The result is a site that feels like a modern SaaS product, not a static marketing brochure — and that alone is a differentiator in a category where even the biggest vendors still feel like the year 2008.

If your product is sharp, your website can't be dull.
