Saffron & Salt

01 / The challenge

Fire-forward food.
A website that wasn't.

Saffron & Salt has been one of the most quietly loved restaurants in Le Marais since 2019. Chef-driven Levantine cooking, born over charcoal, seasonal and generous, served in a room that feels like the best dinner party in the 4th arrondissement. The food is loud in the best way — charred lamb, tahini, pickled onion, dishes that arrive at the table still smelling of fire. The website, in contrast, was silent. A single PDF-style menu, a "book a table" link that opened an email, and a photo gallery so dark and small that even the best dishes looked unappetizing. For a restaurant of this calibre, the site needed to do two jobs at once. First, it had to make a visitor hungry before they'd ever walked through the door. Second, it had to convert — bookings, event enquiries, and a working takeaway channel — without ever feeling like a corporate hospitality site. That tension between appetite and transaction is what the design had to solve.

02 / The approach

Born over fire.
Designed to say so.

We built the direction around the fire. The palette is deep pomegranate red, saffron gold, and charred herb green — colors that come straight out of the kitchen and the Levant, not out of a hospitality template. Playfair Display for headings gives the menu the editorial presence of a cookbook or a food magazine, while Karla keeps the interface warm and readable. The decorative dividers are drawn as small spice motifs in inline SVG — cardamom pods, fennel fronds, dried chilies — because the design should feel like it belongs in the same room as the food. The menu itself is the centerpiece. A tabbed interface (Mezze / From the Fire / Mains / Sweets / Drinks) lets the restaurant present the full menu without overwhelming a first-time visitor, and each dish carries its description, price, and dietary tags in one clean line. Booking and events each get their own dedicated page rather than living as a secondary tab — because for a restaurant of this calibre, a private dinner enquiry is not a footnote, it's a serious revenue channel.

Warm, fire-forward design system with spice-motif illustrations

Tabbed menu with dietary tags and honest descriptions

Reservations and events as first-class pages, not afterthoughts

03 / The build

Menu that works.
Bookings that happen.

The build is a static site with a working tabbed menu, a rotating pull-quote on the homepage, and a reservations form with client-side validation and a clear success message. The events page includes three private-dining packages (The Long Table, The Chef's Table, The Whole Room) with capacity and per-head pricing, plus its own inquiry form. The contact page consolidates hours, location, and a stylized inline-SVG map of Le Marais — no third-party maps, no tracking scripts. Motion is restrained but warm. The homepage hero carries a subtle Ken Burns zoom on the primary photograph. The tabbed menu crossfades between categories. The pull-quote band rotates slowly. Everything respects prefers-reduced-motion, and nothing gets in the way of the food, the menu, or the booking button.

Hunger is a design problem. Solve it honestly.
