Velure

01 / The challenge

Good products.
Bad first impression.

Clean beauty is a crowded, noisy, and often unserious category. Every week another brand launches with the same visual language: pastel gradients, handwritten typography, "self-care" copy that treats the customer like a child, and the word "nourish" used roughly eleven times per page. For a brand that actually formulates seriously — dermatologist-tested, ingredient-transparent, cruelty-free, refill-friendly — that aesthetic actively undermines the product. Velure had the harder problem. The formulations were genuinely good, the ingredient list was honest, and the packaging already looked considered. But the existing site read like every other D2C cosmetics brand, which meant it read like nothing. The brief was clear: build a storefront that feels like the product actually is — quiet, ingredient-led, and quietly expensive. No "clean beauty" clichés. No pastel gradients. No copy that sounds like it was written by the same AI that wrote everyone else's.

02 / The approach

Editorial, not e-commerce.
Ingredient-led, not marketing-led.

We built the site around three positioning decisions. First, the visual language is editorial, not retail. Warm porcelain backgrounds, deep plum typography, an elegant serif for headings (Cormorant Garamond) paired with a geometric sans (Jost) for the interface. It reads like a magazine spread or a skincare journal, not like a Shopify theme. Second, ingredients are the hero. Every product page includes a full ingredient explanation, an ingredient glossary, and molecule-style inline illustrations — because the ingredient list is the product story for this brand. Third, the shopping experience is routine-led. Instead of a wall of products, the site guides customers through Cleanse → Treat → Hydrate → Protect, and gives them a shop-by-concern path (hydration, brightening, barrier repair, anti-aging) that maps to how they actually think about their skin. The result is a site that behaves like a well-edited magazine and a well-built shop at the same time — because for a premium brand, those two things should never have been separate in the first place.

03 / The build

Fast, filterable,
quietly luxurious.

The site is built as a static storefront — HTML, CSS, and vanilla JavaScript only — with a working product filter, a sort dropdown, an image gallery on the product detail page, an accordion for ingredient breakdowns, and a toast confirmation on "Add to bag." None of it requires a backend, a framework, or a build step. The filtering actually works. The gallery actually swaps images. The accordions actually open. Everything feels like a real D2C site because everything behaves like one. The motion layer is deliberately restrained. The announcement bar rotates. The product images fade in on scroll. The gallery swaps without a heavy transition. The overall impression is of a site that is quietly expensive — not because it moves a lot, but because it moves with purpose. For a clean beauty brand, that's the right register.

Luxury is what's left when you remove everything that isn't necessary.
